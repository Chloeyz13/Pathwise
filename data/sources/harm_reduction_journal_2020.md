# Harm Reduction Journal 2020: Hostility, compassion and role reversal in West Virginia's long opioid overdose emergency
url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7549084

West Virginia has experienced statistically significant increases in drug overdose deaths every year since 2013, with an age-adjusted death rate of 57.8 per 100,000 in 2017. Cabell County had an opioid overdose death rate of 72.7 per 100,000 that year, almost five times the national average.

West Virginia is a rural Appalachian state; its population is approximately 93% Caucasian and the poverty rate is 17.9%.

There has been considerable focus on the high levels of opioid prescribing that preceded the current heroin and fentanyl epidemic. While opioid prescribing rates have fallen nationwide, West Virginia has continued to see higher than average rates of opioid prescriptions.

Demand factors proposed for the high levels of prescription opioid use include out-migration of young adults more resilient to problem drug use, and economic stressors.
